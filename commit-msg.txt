perf(rdp): send the damage the target meant, not the union of it

ironrdp unions every rectangle in a fast-path PDU into one before
handing it over, so a caret top-left and a clock bottom-right arrive as
a single near-full-screen update. Encoding that verbatim is where the
bandwidth went: measured over a minute of scrolling, 75.8 megapixels on
a 1.82 megapixel screen, about 41 full-screen repaints.

That was never a codec problem. At 0.116 bytes per pixel the encoder was
already achieving roughly 26:1 against raw RGB; it was simply being handed
far too many pixels. Both earlier attempts missed this, which is why JPEG
made things worse rather than better.

Each update is now diffed against the previous frame with
ironrdp-graphics' tile diff, and only the rectangles that actually differ
are encoded. The whole advertised area is still copied into the shadow
frame afterwards, or the next comparison would run against stale pixels,
and the shadow is dropped whenever a reactivation rebuilds the
framebuffer.

The report gains offered_megapixels alongside megapixels so the
suppression ratio is visible directly rather than inferred.

Costs one framebuffer of memory, about 7 MB at 1920x950. A tile compare
is far cheaper than the PNG encode it avoids.
